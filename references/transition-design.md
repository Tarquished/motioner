# Transition and interaction design

Use for multi-shot films, product walkthroughs, or a seamless or morphing brief. A transition should carry the viewer's eye and an idea into the next scene.

## Study a relevant reference

When the brief permits research, find actual transition examples online that resemble the film's subject, palette, and pace. Watch the motion, rather than judging a still or search thumbnail. Note the shared form, palette relationship, direction, timing, and sound; adapt the principle without copying a distinctive sequence. Useful starting points are [Motion Design School's morphing study](https://motiondesign.school/blog/morphing-study-how-to-create-animated-transition/), [Adobe's match-cut guide](https://www.adobe.com/in/creativecloud/video/discover/match-cut.html), and [Adobe's discussion of continuity in interface motion](https://blog.adobe.com/en/publish/2019/06/19/designing-animation-six-principles-using-animation-ux). These are examples to inspect, not mandatory visual styles.

## Design the scene pair

For **each** boundary, record the outgoing and incoming subjects, shared carrier, viewer's eye focus, direction, approximate frames, and narrative reason. Sketch the carrier at the start, midpoint, and end with its silhouette, apparent mass, position, scale, color role, and neighboring background. Choose one to three important shared forms rather than trying to morph an entire crowded screen. Animate the large forms first; add detail only after the connection reads.

Make a small palette bridge before polishing motion. Record the actual outgoing and incoming swatches for carrier, background, and accent. Prefer a carrier that belongs plausibly in both scenes, or use a visible intermediate object, mask, lighting change, camera passage, or brand color to explain the shift. Preserve a stable visual anchor while color changes. Check the encoded midpoint for muddy blends, accidental color flashes, vanishing contrast, and a carrier whose hue suddenly appears unrelated. An intentional contrast can work when the action or story explains it; do not smooth away a deliberate impact merely to match hex values. If the color relationship remains incoherent, redesign the pair or use a clean cut.

For a morph, a traceable feature must change shape across intermediate frames. Keep perceptual landmarks stable enough to follow, and match direction and edge speed across the boundary. A dissolve may support the transformation, but opacity alone is not a morph. When shapes cannot plausibly transform, use motivated occlusion or a match cut. Vary methods across the film and give the incoming scene time to settle.

## Prove interactions

For every visible click, tap, drag, or selection, record **target → contact frame → state before → immediate response → state after**. In rendered pixels, the pointer must meet its target, the result must be absent before contact, and an understandable change must begin afterward. Use restrained anticipation, hover, press, lift, follow-through, camera response, or content reveal where helpful. A repeated click ring is not enough variation.

## Judge every handoff after rendering

Review a short loop of **every** morph or seamless boundary and then the whole encoded film at normal speed. Inspect several frames before the boundary, the start, midpoint, end, and several frames after. Record one verdict per handoff:

- **Good:** the shared form remains recognizable, eye path and velocity are coherent, colors feel connected or deliberately motivated, text and contrast survive, and the sound supports the change.
- **Weak:** an arbitrary color jump, muddy midpoint, unrelated background, shape pop, speed hiccup, ghost text, stranded layer, confused focal point, or detached cue draws attention away from the story. Name the exact frames and cause, redesign, rerender, and review again.
- **Unverified:** the encoded sequence or normal-speed playback was unavailable. Do not call it seamless based only on source code or selected stills.

Do not approve a film because the best transition is strong while another handoff remains weak. A numerical color-difference score may help find a discontinuity but cannot decide whether a contrast is aesthetically or narratively right.
