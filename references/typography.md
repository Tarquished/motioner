# Typography direction

For type-led or branded films, start with the product's actual type and identity if available. If there is no required font, shortlist at least two genuinely different candidates with suitable licenses and the needed language glyphs. A candidate should have a reason to exist in this film beyond being installed by default.

Render the same real title, caption, and UI label in each candidate at the final video size and normal playback scale. Compare silhouette, character, x-height, spacing, numeral shapes, diacritics, and how weights pair. Pick one cohesive system, potentially with a contrasting accent face only when it improves hierarchy. Keep text clear under movement, compression, and phone-sized playback.

Record the choice and why the rejected candidate lost. Check font loading in the render pipeline rather than assuming a fallback is acceptable. Prevent layout jumps by measuring or fixing line breaks at the final weight and width. Evaluate moving and settled text in the encoded film, including transition frames; replace a generic-feeling font or treatment when the result lacks a clear identity.
